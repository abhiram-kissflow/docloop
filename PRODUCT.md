# Product

## Register

product

Docloop's dashboard is a working surface, not a pitch. A second, brand-register surface is
anticipated later — an internal page that explains Docloop to other Kissflow teams considering
adopting it. That surface gets its own treatment when it exists; it does not soften this one.

## Platform

web

## Users

The Kissflow documentation team — a lead and four writers — are the people who open this daily.
They are writers and editors: they read for a living, they notice bad type, and they can hold a
lot of text in their heads at once. Density is a courtesy to them, not a burden.

Product managers and engineers are the second audience. They arrive occasionally and without
context, usually because something they shipped broke a doc. A row must be understandable to
someone who has never opened this before and will not be given a tour.

The job to be done: decide what happens to each documentation suggestion the system has produced.

## Product Purpose

Docloop watches Kissflow's product for change — support-ticket patterns, code commits, live UI
drift — and proposes documentation work in response. The dashboard is where a human accepts or
rejects each proposal. Nothing publishes without passing through this screen.

Success is a queue worked to empty in twenty focused minutes, with the reviewer confident about
every call they made. Not time-on-page, not returning visits. The screen is a workload with an
end.

## Positioning

Every other doc-rot tool watches the product's surface. Docloop reads its source — so every
suggestion arrives with the evidence that justifies it already attached.

## Brand Personality

Exact, unhurried, accountable. It speaks the way a good editor speaks: plainly, in finished
sentences, without hedging or exclamation. It never celebrates itself and never apologises for
taking up space with a necessary detail.

The emotional target is confidence at speed. The reviewer should feel the decision is theirs and
that they have everything needed to make it.

## Anti-references

Consumer-app cute — illustrated empty states, pastel rounded everything, emoji, confetti when the
queue clears. This is a professional instrument used daily by people doing serious work; delight
here means a fast, correct decision, not a reward animation.

More broadly, the interface must not read as machine-generated design. That failure is the same
failure Docloop exists to prevent, one layer up.

## Design Principles

**Evidence before verdict.** No suggestion appears without the ticket pattern and the questions
that produced it, visible in the same view. A reviewer should never have to navigate away to
decide, and never have to trust the system's word.

**The machine proposes, the human decides.** Model-authored text and human-approved text are never
visually interchangeable. The reader can always tell which is which, without being told.

**Practice what we preach.** A tool built by a documentation team to enforce documentation quality
has to be impeccably written itself. Every label, empty state, and error message is edited prose.

**Queue to empty.** The layout serves getting through the work and closing the tab. Nothing is
present to be admired, monitored, or explored for its own sake.

**Legible cold.** A product manager opening this once, unbriefed, understands what a row is
claiming and what their options are.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at or above 4.5:1, large text at or above 3:1. Visible focus indication on
every interactive element. The entire triage path — move through the queue, read the evidence,
approve, dismiss — is completable from the keyboard alone. All motion has a
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` alternative.
